Particle accelerators · a primer

Machines that turn fields into controlled beams.

Particle accelerators use electric fields to add energy to charged particles and magnets to focus and steer them. Flagship accelerators are billion-dollar scientific facilities, with thousands of components that must work in concert.

The LANSCE linear accelerator at Los Alamos stretches about one kilometer, while CERN’s Large Hadron Collider follows a 27-kilometer ring. Along every beamline, instruments continuously measure position, intensity, timing, and losses so operators can keep the machine stable and safe.

The challenge

Why standard fixed-channel assumptions break down in facility-scale telemetry.

Accelerator data are not one tidy multivariate time series.

A channel’s meaning comes from its sensor, physical location, and subsystem—not its column index. The observed channels can change between acquisitions, while each subsystem evolves on its own time scale.

01

Variable sensor availability

Channels range from one to 1,754 in the LANSCE corpus, with no fixed global layout required by the model.

02

Multiple temporal scales

Fast RF and beam events coexist with slower vacuum, cooling, and power-supply behavior.

03

Missing and noisy measurements

Isolated dropouts, missing intervals, unavailable channels, and structured gaps all occur in operation.

04

Operating drift

Configurations, calibration, component aging, and operating conditions shift the sensor distribution over time.

The method

Set-aware in channels.
Attentive through time.

SensOFormer combines permutation-invariant sensor encoding, latent temporal processing, and permutation-equivariant reconstruction. The result is one architecture that accommodates different channel sets and sequence structures without channel-dimension padding.

01

Mask + denoise

Corrupt visible telemetry with point, time, channel, or block masks plus sampled Gaussian noise.

02

Encode the set

Learnable latent queries cross-attend to channel tokens, independent of their input ordering.

03

Model temporal context

A latent transformer processes the compact sequence to learn structure across time.

04

Decode or adapt

Reconstruct queried channels or attach a lightweight head for binary and multiclass tasks.

Scaling behavior

More capacity and more data lower reconstruction loss.

The scaling study asks whether SensOFormer continues to learn better representations as the backbone and pretraining corpus grow. Across five model sizes and four data fractions, validation masked-reconstruction loss decreases along both axes.

The paper summarizes this behavior with a separable power law: one term captures limits from model capacity, while a second captures limits from available pretraining data.

General law · Eq. 7

L(N, D) = L∞ + ANα + BDβ

N is parameter count, D is pretraining-data size, and L∞ is the fitted asymptotic loss.

SensOFormer fit · Eq. 8

L(N, D) = 0.02469 + 1.097 × 102N0.715 + 5.267 × 10−1D0.374

The fit uses raw counts for N and D and has RMSE 4 × 10−4 over the observed configurations.

2.38–80.54Mparameters across five model variants
12.5–100%of the pretraining corpus across four data scales

Interpretation. This is evidence of improvement within the evaluated grid—not a guarantee that the same power law extrapolates beyond it. The practical message is to grow model capacity and accelerator data together.

Pretraining data

Heterogeneity is the training signal.

SensOFormer learns from five accelerator data families without forcing them into one global channel schema. Each source keeps its own sensors, sequence structure, and operational context.

Five-source corpus

  • 01LANSCEOperational telemetry · LANL4,250 records · 1–1,754 ch.
  • 02LHC-BLBeam-loss monitoring · CERN2 long sequences · 66 ch.
  • 03HVCM-SNSPower-electronics waveforms · ORNL10,000 samples · 12 ch.
  • 04RFS-LCLSRF-station signals · SLAC9,841 records · 9 ch.
  • 05SRF-CEBAFSuperconducting-RF events · JLab2,375 events · 192 steps

From sequence to training example

  1. Split first

    Original sequences are divided 90/10 before windowing, so overlapping windows cannot leak across train and validation sets.

  2. Window

    Each sequence becomes a 1,024-step window with stride 256; channel counts remain dataset-specific with no channel padding.

  3. Normalize

    Visible values are scaled per window and per channel, using no statistics from the masked targets.

  4. Denoise

    Noise is added only to visible measurements, teaching the model to recover structure instead of copying inputs.

Mask bank · one pattern sampled per window

Point-wiseHide 15% of individual values
Time-wiseHide 75% of channels at one time
Channel-wiseHide 60% of one sensor’s time steps
Block-wiseHide a contiguous ≈15% time–channel region

Together, the masks reproduce isolated dropouts, missing time slices, intermittent sensors, and structured gaps seen in accelerator telemetry.

Downstream tasks

One representation, three modes of use.

The pretrained encoder and temporal processor form the shared backbone. SensOFormer either reuses its reconstruction decoder directly or adds a compact task-specific head.

01

Zero-shot · reconstruction

Missing-data imputation

Recover masked sensor values from temporal and cross-channel context across six held-out test settings. No downstream labels or task-specific training are required.

Frozen backbone · binary head

Anomaly detection

Pretrained backbone · multiclass heads

Cavity & fault identification

Separate lightweight heads identify the first faulting CEBAF cavity across 9 classes and the fault type across 8 classes. The fault benchmark is surpassed with only 25% of the labeled training set.
