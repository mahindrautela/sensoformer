The challenge

Why standard fixed-channel assumptions break down in facility-scale telemetry.

Accelerator data are not one tidy multivariate time series.

A channel’s meaning comes from its sensor, physical location, and subsystem—not its column index. The observed channels can change between acquisitions, while each subsystem evolves on its own time scale.

01

Variable sensor availability

Channels range from one to 1,754 in the LANSCE corpus, with no fixed global layout required by the model.

02

Multiple temporal scales

Fast RF and beam events coexist with slower vacuum, cooling, and power-supply behavior.

03

Missing and noisy measurements

Isolated dropouts, missing intervals, unavailable channels, and structured gaps all occur in operation.

04

Operating drift

Configurations, calibration, component aging, and operating conditions shift the sensor distribution over time.

Scaling behavior

More capacity and more data lower reconstruction loss.

The paper summarizes this behavior with a separable power law: one term captures limits from model capacity, while a second captures limits from available pretraining data.

General law · Eq. 7

L(N, D) = L∞ + ANα + BDβ

N is parameter count, D is pretraining-data size, and L∞ is the fitted asymptotic loss.

SensOFormer fit · Eq. 8

L(N, D) = 0.02469 + 1.097 × 102N0.715 + 5.267 × 10−1D0.374

The fit uses raw counts for N and D and has RMSE 4 × 10−4 over the observed configurations.

2.38–80.54Mparameters across five model variants
12.5–100%of the pretraining corpus across four data scales

Interpretation. This is evidence of improvement within the evaluated grid—not a guarantee that the same power law extrapolates beyond it. The practical message is to grow model capacity and accelerator data together.

From sequence to training example

  1. Split first

    Original sequences are divided 90/10 before windowing, so overlapping windows cannot leak across train and validation sets.

  2. Window

    Each sequence becomes a 1,024-step window with stride 256; channel counts remain dataset-specific with no channel padding.

  3. Normalize

    Visible values are scaled per window and per channel, using no statistics from the masked targets.

  4. Denoise

    Noise is added only to visible measurements, teaching the model to recover structure instead of copying inputs.

Downstream tasks

One representation, three modes of use.

The pretrained encoder and temporal processor form the shared backbone. SensOFormer either reuses its reconstruction decoder directly or adds a compact task-specific head.

01

Zero-shot · reconstruction

Missing-data imputation

Recover masked sensor values from temporal and cross-channel context across seven evaluation datasets spanning six facilities. No downstream labels or task-specific training are required.

02

Frozen backbone · binary head

Anomaly detection

A lightweight temporal CNN head learns normal-versus-faulty HVCM-SNS behavior while the pretrained encoder and processor remain frozen. Scaling the backbone from XTi to B improves early warning.
