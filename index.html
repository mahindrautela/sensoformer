SensOFormer: Foundation Models for Particle Accelerators.

To our knowledge, the first foundation model for particle-accelerator telemetry.

A self-supervised transformer learns one shared representation from five accelerators—even as sensor sets change—and transfers it to reconstruction and operational diagnostics.

Research at a glance

From fragmented telemetry to a shared operational representation.

01

Problem

Every facility speaks a different sensor language.

Channel sets, sample rates, sequence lengths, physical units, and operating conditions vary across accelerator subsystems and acquisitions.

→
02

Idea

Model channels as a set, then learn through time.

A Perceiver-style encoder maps variable sensor sets into a shared latent sequence; a transformer captures temporal dependencies.

→
03

Evidence

Reuse the backbone across four operational tasks.

The pretrained representation supports zero-shot reconstruction and lightweight supervised adaptation for accelerator diagnostics.

The challenge

Why standard fixed-channel assumptions break down in facility-scale telemetry.

Accelerator data are not one tidy multivariate time series.

A channel’s meaning comes from its sensor, physical location, and subsystem—not its column index. The observed channels can change between acquisitions, while each subsystem evolves on its own time scale.

01

Variable sensor availability

Channels range from one to 1,754 in the LANSCE corpus, with no fixed global layout required by the model.

02

Multiple temporal scales

Fast RF and beam events coexist with slower vacuum, cooling, and power-supply behavior.

03

Missing and noisy measurements

Isolated dropouts, missing intervals, unavailable channels, and structured gaps all occur in operation.

04

Operating drift

Configurations, calibration, component aging, and operating conditions shift the sensor distribution over time.

The method

Set-aware in channels.
Attentive through time.

SensOFormer combines permutation-invariant sensor encoding, latent temporal processing, and permutation-equivariant reconstruction. The result is one architecture that accommodates different channel sets and sequence structures without channel-dimension padding.

01

Mask + denoise

Corrupt visible telemetry with point, time, channel, or block masks plus sampled Gaussian noise.

02

Encode the set

Learnable latent queries cross-attend to channel tokens, independent of their input ordering.

03

Model temporal context

A latent transformer processes the compact sequence to learn structure across time.

04

Decode or adapt

Reconstruct queried channels or attach a lightweight head for binary and multiclass tasks.

Scaling behavior

More capacity and more data lower reconstruction loss.

The scaling study asks whether SensOFormer continues to learn better representations as the backbone and pretraining corpus grow. Across five model sizes and four data fractions, validation masked-reconstruction loss decreases along both axes.

The paper summarizes this behavior with a separable power law: one term captures limits from model capacity, while a second captures limits from available pretraining data.

General law · Eq. 7

L(N, D) = L∞ + ANα + BDβ

N is parameter count, D is pretraining-data size, and L∞ is the fitted asymptotic loss.

SensOFormer fit · Eq. 8

L(N, D) = 0.02469 + 1.097 × 102N0.715 + 5.267 × 10−1D0.374

The fit uses raw counts for N and D and has RMSE 4 × 10−4 over the observed configurations.

2.38–80.54Mparameters across five model variants
12.5–100%of the pretraining corpus across four data scales

Interpretation. This is evidence of improvement within the evaluated grid—not a guarantee that the same power law extrapolates beyond it. The practical message is to grow model capacity and accelerator data together.

What the study shows

A credible first step toward reusable models for accelerator operations.

01

Sensor sets remove a rigid schema assumption.

Permutation-invariant encoding and equivariant decoding let one architecture process different channel collections without imposing a facility-wide column layout.

02

Masked denoising aligns pretraining with real missingness.

The objective explicitly covers isolated points, partial time slices, intermittent channels, and structured time–channel gaps.

03

The representation transfers beyond reconstruction.

Frozen-backbone adaptation is evaluated on binary anomaly detection and multiclass cavity and fault identification—not only on the pretraining task.

04

More capacity and more data both help in the tested regime.

The scaling result is an empirical diagnostic within the measured grid, not a claim that the fitted law will extrapolate indefinitely.
